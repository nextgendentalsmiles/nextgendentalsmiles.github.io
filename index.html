Dental Implants

Dental implants are designed to replace missing teeth. An implant is placed in the jawbone to support a dental crown, bridge or implant-retained denture. Depending on the patient’s condition, implants may be considered for one missing tooth, multiple teeth or a complete dental arch.

Implant treatment begins with an evaluation of the teeth, gums, bite and available bone. Smoking, gum disease, certain health conditions and insufficient bone may affect treatment planning or healing. Some patients may require additional procedures before an implant can be placed.

Emergency Dentistry

Severe tooth pain, facial swelling, dental trauma, a broken tooth, uncontrolled bleeding or a knocked-out permanent tooth may require prompt professional attention. Delaying treatment can sometimes allow pain, infection or damage to become more serious.

A knocked-out permanent tooth should be handled carefully by the crown rather than the root. Patients should avoid attempting complicated treatment at home. Facial swelling accompanied by difficulty breathing or swallowing requires urgent medical attention.

Cosmetic Dentistry

Cosmetic dentistry focuses on improving the appearance of the teeth and smile while considering oral health and function. Treatment options may include professional teeth whitening, porcelain veneers, dental bonding or a customized combination of procedures.

Before cosmetic treatment, existing problems such as tooth decay, gum disease or an unhealthy bite may need attention. Results vary according to the condition of the teeth, treatment selected and the patient’s maintenance habits.

Root Canal Treatment

Root canal treatment may be recommended when the tissue inside a tooth becomes inflamed or infected. Deep decay, cracks, dental trauma and repeated treatment on the same tooth are among the conditions that can affect the dental pulp.

During treatment, affected tissue is removed and the inside of the tooth is cleaned, shaped and sealed. A crown or another restoration may subsequently be needed to protect the tooth and restore its function. Only an examination can determine whether root canal treatment is appropriate.

Children’s Dental Care

Regular dental visits help monitor the development of a child’s teeth and allow potential problems to be identified early. A comfortable and age-appropriate approach can also help children develop positive attitudes toward professional dental care.

Children’s dental services may include examinations, professional cleanings, fluoride treatments, sealants, fillings and guidance about brushing, flossing and nutrition. Recommendations should be based on the child’s age, development and individual cavity risk.

Parents can learn more about visiting a kids dentist in Vacaville .

General and Preventive Dental Care

Preventive care is intended to help maintain healthy teeth and gums and identify changes before they require more complicated treatment. Routine care may include dental examinations, professional cleanings, appropriate X-rays, gum assessments and oral cancer screenings.

The recommended frequency of dental visits is not identical for every patient. People with active gum disease, a high cavity risk or certain health concerns may require a schedule different from someone with stable oral health.
